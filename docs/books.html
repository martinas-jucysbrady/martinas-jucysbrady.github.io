Books

1984

George Orwell

A scary and brilliant example of what society could become. Orwell's vision of surveillance, propaganda and the erosion of truth feels disturbingly relevant — the kind of book that stays with you long after you've finished it.

★★★★★ 5 / 5

Life of Pi

Yann Martel

A gripping story with a fascinating premise, but one of those rare cases where the film was actually better. The book has its moments, but the visuals and pacing of the adaptation brought the story to life in a way the pages couldn't quite match.

★★★★★ 3.5 / 5

Pet Sematary

Stephen King

Interesting writing and an unsettling premise, but not entirely my cup of tea. King clearly has a distinctive style and there is craft here — it just didn't fully connect with me personally.

★★★★★ 3.5 / 5

Master & Apprentice

Claudia Gray — Star Wars

Provides a brilliant look into the true story behind the relationship between Qui-Gon and Obi-Wan. For any Star Wars fan this is essential reading — it adds real depth to characters you thought you already knew well.

★★★★★ 4.5 / 5

The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo / Played with Fire / Kicked the Hornet's Nest

Stieg Larsson — Millennium Trilogy (Books 1–3)

Absolutely amazing writing — emotional, gripping and brilliantly constructed. Larsson had a rare ability to pull you completely into his world, and Lisbeth Salander is one of the most compelling characters in modern fiction. The original three are as good as crime fiction gets.

★★★★★ 5 / 5

The Girl in the Spider's Web / Takes an Eye for an Eye / Lived Twice

David Lagercrantz — Millennium (Books 4–6)

Nothing compares to Larsson, but Lagercrantz has done a respectable job of continuing the story. The essence of the series is mostly preserved and there is enough here to satisfy fans of the originals, even if some of the spark is inevitably missing.

★★★★★ 4 / 5

The Girl in the Eagle's Talons

Karin Smirnoff — Millennium (Book 7)

Two stars is generous. A disappointing change in writing style compared to everything that came before it. When you pick up a book you want to escape the real world, but this author introduced a kind of real-world agenda into the story that pulled you straight back out of it. A shame.

★★★★★ 2 / 5

Anna Karenina

Leo Tolstoy

Very long and very in-depth, but beautifully written. Tolstoy takes his time with everything — the characters, the social world, the inner lives of his cast — and the patience it demands is repaid. A genuine classic that earns that label.

★★★★★ 4 / 5

Divergent / Insurgent / Allegiant / Four

Veronica Roth — Divergent Series

Just a very good series of books. The faction system is a clever premise and the story maintains its momentum well across all four entries. Roth builds a world you genuinely invest in and the characters carry it through to the end.

★★★★★ 4 / 5

Never Let Me Go

Kazuo Ishiguro

The first half was quite difficult to get through — nothing really hooked me in. But as the story grew and I had more of an understanding of the world, it became increasingly mysterious and interesting. In the end it is a very sad book. I would have rated it higher if the beginning had been written better.

★★★★★ 3 / 5

The Finisher / The Keeper / The Width of the World / The Stars Below

David Baldacci — Vega Jane Series

Honestly this series did surprise me with how good it actually was. Really well done by the author. The world Baldacci builds is imaginative and the story pulls you along in a way you don't quite expect going in. A genuinely impressive achievement.

★★★★★ 4.5 / 5

Meditations

Marcus Aurelius

An amazing look into the mind of such a significant historical figure. What strikes you most is how little has changed — the same anxieties, the same temptations, the same search for meaning. Reading it feels like a reminder that the fundamentals of being human are timeless.

★★★★★ 4 / 5

The Hunger Games / Catching Fire / Mockingjay

Suzanne Collins — The Hunger Games Trilogy

An amazing story from start to finish. Collins builds a brutal world that never feels gratuitous, and Katniss is a protagonist worth following through all three books. The series maintains its quality throughout and the ending earns its emotional weight.

★★★★★ 4.5 / 5

A Thousand Splendid Suns

Khaled Hosseini

An amazing and deeply sad story. Hosseini writes about suffering and endurance with extraordinary care, and the bond between the two central characters carries enormous emotional weight. Not an easy read, but a very rewarding one.

★★★★★ 4 / 5

Harry Potter (Books 1–7)

J.K. Rowling — Harry Potter Series

Well worth the read, every single one of them. The world Rowling built is endlessly inventive and the series grows with you — the later books are darker and richer in a way that feels completely earned. One of those rare series where the whole is even greater than the sum of its parts.

★★★★★ 5 / 5

A Gentleman in Moscow

Amor Towles

A well-written and elegantly constructed novel. Count Rostov's decades under hotel house arrest is a slow burn, but Towles writes with warmth and wit that you settle into comfortably. A pleasant and thoughtful read.

★★★★★ 3 / 5
